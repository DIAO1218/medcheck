# Candidate Evaluation Table

Purpose: justify the architecture chosen in `docs/architecture/adr/001-deterministic-core-model-at-the-edges.md` with numbers rather than taste. Scores are 1–5 (5 = best) assigned by the plan agent, then challenged in review; the challenge is recorded in `docs/architecture/evaluation-logbook.md`.

---

## 1. Criteria and weights

| # | Criterion | Weight | Why this weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | **Invariant provability** — can each zero-tolerance invariant be proven offline, with a test that fails when it is violated? | **×3** | The course requires zero-tolerance evidence. This property cannot be bought back later; everything else can be improved incrementally. |
| C2 | **Auditability / traceability** — can every output be traced to a rule and a table version? | ×2 | Driver D3. A verdict nobody can explain is not a verdict. |
| C3 | **Recall on messy text** — proportion of realistic notes it can handle without human escalation | ×2 | Real safety value, but failures here degrade to `HOLD`, which is a cost, not a catastrophe. |
| C4 | **Cost per case** | ×1 | Bounded by REQ-C-001 ($0.02). A hard cliff, not a continuum. |
| C5 | **Latency** | ×1 | Bounded by REQ-P-001/002. Also a cliff. |
| C6 | **Build and maintenance effort** | ×1 | We have one semester and 3–4 people. |
| C7 | **Offline CI feasibility** — can the regression and semantic gates run without a live model? | ×2 | Driver D6; gate 5 is otherwise theatre. |

Maximum score = 5 × (3+2+2+1+1+1+2) = **60**. The ×3 on C1 and ×2 on C7 encode the two properties we refuse to trade: *provability* and *CI that can actually block*.

## 2. Candidate scores

| Criterion (weight) | A1 single call w/ structured output | A2 LLM panel (propose–critique–judge) | A3 RAG over tables decides | **A4 deterministic core, model at edges** | A5 fine-tuned classifier |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 Invariant provability (×3) | 1 | 1 | 2 | **5** | 3 |
| C2 Auditability (×2) | 1 | 1 | 3 | **5** | 2 |
| C3 Recall on messy text (×2) | 5 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 4 |
| C4 Cost per case (×1) | 5 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 4 |
| C5 Latency (×1) | 5 | 2 | 3 | **5** | 5 |
| C6 Build effort (×1) | 5 | 3 | 2 | 3 | 1 |
| C7 Offline CI (×2) | 1 | 1 | 1 | **5** | 2 |
| **Weighted total / 60** | **32** | **24** | **30** | **53** | **35** |

### Justifications for the contested cells

- **A1 / C1 = 1.** Not "low" — *zero*. With a model-signed verdict there is no offline test whose failure proves an invariant broken; the test would only prove the model's mood.
- **A1 / C7 = 1.** Every regression run would need live calls or recorded tapes. A tape-based test verifies the tape.
- **A2 / C4 = 2.** 3 calls × ~600 tokens ≈ $0.06/case, 3× the REQ-C-001 ceiling.
- **A3 / C1 = 2.** Grounding improves plausibility, but retrieval *misses* are silent false negatives — the worst failure mode under driver D1. Rejected as a decision path, retained as a perception aid (ADR §3).
- **A4 / C3 = 3.** Honest score: prose the extraction grammar cannot express becomes `COVERAGE_GAP`. We accept more holds.
- **A4 / C6 = 3.** Two artefacts (extraction contract + tables) instead of one prompt, plus invariant suites. Real cost, paid once.
- **A5 / C6 = 1.** No labelled training corpus, no annotation budget, no evaluation set at the required scale. Dropped in round 1.

## 3. Sensitivity analysis

The result must not depend on a single weighting choice, or it is numerology.

| Scenario | A1 | A2 | A3 | **A4** | A5 | Winner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (as above) | 32 | 24 | 30 | **53** | 35 | A4 |
| C1 weight ×5 (provability doubled again) | 38 | 28 | 34 | **61** | 41 | A4 |
| C3 weight ×4 (recall tripled — "safety means catching everything") | 42 | 34 | 38 | **59** | 47 | A4 |
| C6 weight ×3 (effort tripled — "we're out of time") | 42 | 30 | 34 | **57** | 37 | A4 |
| C1, C2, C7 all set to ×1 (only "engineering" criteria) | 26 | 19 | 24 | **35** | 27 | A4 |
| C1 weight = 0 **and** C3 weight ×4 | 39 | 33 | 35 | 32 | 43 | A5 |

A4 wins under every weighting that keeps provability as a criterion at all. It loses only in the last row, where the safety driver is deleted from the model — and that is a change of drivers, not a change of scores. If a future team genuinely abandons driver D1 ("a wrong approve is unacceptable"), this table must be recomputed and a new ADR written; the table is designed to make that choice explicit rather than accidental.

## 4. What would change the answer

| Evidence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Extraction field accuracy < 80% on accepted inputs after two prompt iterations | A4's C3 falls to 1–2; the deterministic core's inputs are too noisy to be worth it → reopen ADR (`condition 2`) |
| Coverage-gap rate stuck > 20% despite table expansion | The closed-world assumption fails → reconsider a hybrid where the *table* is generated by a model but *reviewed and pinned* by humans (still deterministic at runtime) |
| A requirement for probabilistic risk scoring | Categorical verdict architecture no longer fits → new ADR, likely A5 with a calibrated model plus a deterministic override |
| Model pricing drops 20× | C4/C5 stop discriminating; A2 becomes affordable but still non-provable → does not change the winner |

## 5. Non-scored considerations

Two things were decisive and deliberately kept **out** of the score, because scoring them would launder a judgement call:

1. **Pedagogical fit.** The course is about auditable supervision of an agent. A4 produces an artefact trail (contract → tables → verdict → audit) that a reader can audit line by line. A1 produces a transcript of confidence.
2. **Honesty of the failure mode.** Under A4, the system's bad days look like a queue of `HOLD`s. Under A1, they look like a smooth stream of approvals — including the wrong ones. We prefer failures we can see.
